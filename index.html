Plus Ultra 1.0

E-class model rocket: design, fabrication, and flight test

Date
May 2026
Tools
SolidWorks, OpenRocket, Bambu Lab P1S
Apogee
Predicted1,227 ft
Measured1,064 ft
Difference13.3%

A custom E-class rocket taken through a full design cycle: simulation, CAD, 3D printing, assembly, and a flight test measured by an onboard altimeter.

What I did

  • Simulation before hardware. I used OpenRocket stability and trajectory runs to set fin geometry, body tube length, nose cone shape, and mass distribution. The same runs checked off-rail velocity, deployment timing, and descent rate before anything was built.
  • CAD built for printing. I replicated the design as a SolidWorks assembly. Through-the-wall fins with a friction fit keep alignment during assembly, and a custom motor mount holds both the fins and the motor. Every printed part was designed with peak flight loads in mind.
  • Assembly and recovery. I anchored the shock cord for peak deployment loads and packed the parachute with wadding for a clean deployment from the motor's ejection charge.

Result

The flight was nominal from liftoff through recovery. Measured apogee came in 13.3% below the prediction. The likely causes are weathercocking, parasitic drag the model didn't capture, and variation in the motor's actual total impulse.

3-DOF flight simulator

First-principles trajectory model with Monte Carlo landing dispersion

Date
August 2026
Tools
Python, NumPy, Matplotlib, PyYAML, pytest
Apogee, Plus Ultra 1.0
OpenRocket376 m
My simulator372.2 m
Difference1.0%

A point-mass flight simulator written from scratch in Python. I validated it against OpenRocket on Plus Ultra 1.0, then ran it 1,000 times with randomized conditions to predict where the rocket lands.

What I did

  • Physics. Gravity, thrust, drag, and wind act on a point mass in East, North, and altitude coordinates, with thrust and wind each resolved by magnitude and azimuth. Thrust comes from real motor curves loaded from .eng files.
  • Atmosphere. I built a three-layer International Standard Atmosphere from first principles instead of using a lookup table, and checked it against reference density values.
  • Recovery. Ballistic, single-deployment, and dual-deployment modes. Apogee and altitude-triggered events are detected during integration, from the velocity sign change and an altitude threshold, rather than found after the fact.
  • Monte Carlo. 1,000 runs per rocket, randomizing wind speed, wind direction, launch angles, and drag coefficient. Wind-speed spread comes from each launch site's reported sustained-versus-gust spread, so gustier days produce wider landing footprints.
  • Structure and validation. The simulation is one callable function, and each rocket lives in its own YAML file, so new designs need no code changes. I cross-checked ascent against an independent OpenRocket run and confirmed the thrust and wind decomposition by hand calculation.

Result

The simulator matched OpenRocket's apogee for Plus Ultra 1.0 on an Estes E12 to within 1.0%. The Monte Carlo run characterized both apogee variance and the landing footprint, the same kind of dispersion workflow used in range-safety analysis.

Turbojet cycle analysis

Ideal Brayton cycle benchmarked against the Pratt & Whitney F100

Date
December 2025
Tools
Excel, technical report
Net thrust
Dry61.4 kN
Afterburner95.3 kN

An ideal air-standard Brayton cycle worked stage by stage through the inlet, compressor, combustor, turbine, and nozzle, with and without afterburner, then compared to published F100 performance data.

What I did

  • State calculations at every stage. Temperatures and pressures through each compressor stage, the combustor, each turbine stage, and the nozzle.
  • Afterburner case. I added an afterburning configuration to quantify its effect on thrust, a 55% increase over the dry case.
  • Diagrams and report. I plotted the T–s and P–v diagrams in Excel and documented the analysis in a formal technical report.

Result

Predicted thrust landed within about 10% of published reference data, reasonable agreement for an air-standard model of a real engine.
